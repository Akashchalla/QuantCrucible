New to trading? Open the glossary

8-K: a public company filing announcing an important event.

Buyback: permission for a company to repurchase its shares. It does not promise that purchases will happen.

Put: an option that lets its buyer sell shares at a fixed price. Selling a put creates an obligation to buy those shares.

Premium: money received for that obligation. It is not guaranteed profit.

Strike / collateral: the agreed purchase price / cash reserved to pay for the shares. One ordinary contract covers 100 shares.

Control: a comparison date for the same company, chosen without looking at its later profit.

Basis point (bps): 0.01%. A 100 bps return on $10,000 is $100.

Bid / ask: quoted prices to sell / buy. Crossing this gap costs money.

Haircut: an extra pessimistic adjustment to the option price, beyond the bid/ask spread.

Horizon / delay: trading sessions from the filing anchor until exit / until entry.

Development / held-out test: data used to design the method / a later window opened after freezing it.

Uncertainty interval: a rough range for the estimated difference. If it crosses zero, this analysis does not establish a positive edge.

Drawdown: the account's fall from its earlier peak. Sharpe compares average returns with their variability; it does not measure every risk.

Assignment: the option seller must buy the shares at the strike. This can occur before expiry.
